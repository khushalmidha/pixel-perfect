# Steady: five-person Starting Point evaluation

**Status: planned. No participant sessions or results are reported.**

Steady uses three answers to suggest educational content. This study tests whether a first-time investor understands that distinction, finds useful explanations and can continue learning. It does not validate financial suitability, readiness or investment outcomes.

## Setup and participants

Recruit five Indian participants aged approximately 20–26; aim for at least four who have not independently selected a mutual fund. Include different study/work situations. Avoid participants familiar with the build. Record prior experience without collecting personal financial details.

Allow 30–35 minutes per session and roughly four hours for setup, sessions and initial synthesis. Use phones where possible; record browser/device and disclose any desktop simulation. Use the same submitted version and a fresh browser context per participant. Keep that context through refresh checks.

Ask permission before recording; notes alone are sufficient. Use P1–P5, permit withdrawal and do not collect identifying financial data. Dry-run the script without counting it as participant evidence.

## Fictional profiles

Give each participant a profile before starting the timer. Do not tell them its expected path.

| Participants | Profile                                                                                         | Moderator-only expected path                    |

| P1, P4       | Almost nothing left after essentials; no emergency savings; would want to withdraw after a loss | Cash basics: checklist, then risk               |
| P2, P5       | ₹5,000–₹15,000 left; about a month of emergency savings; uneasy about a loss but would wait     | Risk and access: risk, then dips                |
| P3           | ₹5,000–₹15,000 left; three or more months of emergency savings; feels able to handle a loss     | Fund basics: index-fund example, then checklist |

These are content-routing scenarios, not assessments of the participant. Timing with supplied answers does not measure the effort of reflecting on real finances. Branch samples are 2/2/1, not comparable groups.

## Opening and baseline

Read: “We are testing the design, not you. Use this fictional profile; you do not need to share your personal finances. No real payment will happen. Please say what you are looking for or expecting. I will mostly watch. You can stop whenever you like.”

Before showing the app, ask:

- Have you independently chosen a mutual fund before?
- How confident are you in explaining basic investment risks and asking useful questions before a decision? 1 = not confident; 5 = very confident.
- In your own words, what might go wrong if invested money is needed for a bill next week? Record without teaching or correcting yet.

The opening discloses no payments. Later recognition of that fact is not evidence of unaided discovery.

After tasks, give a new scenario: “An investment has fallen and the money might be needed soon. What would you want to understand before deciding what to do?” Ask for reasoning, not an investment decision. Compare with baseline cautiously: tasks and moderator prompts themselves may teach.

## Moderation and recording

Use neutral prompts: “What are you looking for?” and “What did you expect?” Do not praise correct answers or point to controls. Record any prompt. Allow about two minutes per task and four minutes for a lesson. At 180 seconds on onboarding record the target missed, but allow up to four minutes to finish.

Record independent performance before offering navigation help. If a previous task blocks the next, provide facilitator setup and mark it; do not erase the initial failure.

Use U = independent success including comprehension, A = assisted, F = unsuccessful or key misconception retained, N/A = check unavailable. Record navigation and comprehension separately. For each task capture time, code, applicable checks, help, wrong turns and a quote. Record participant ID, experience, device and exact prototype version.

## Post-test questions

1. Repeat the same 1–5 confidence question. What caused any change?
2. How did Steady choose where you began? What did it not decide?
3. How pressured did you feel to invest? 1 = none; 5 = strong. Which words caused it?
4. How clear were risks? 1–5. What could go wrong with the category you explored?
5. What does completing a lesson mean? What were the Journey numbers?
6. What was confusing, unnecessary or missing? Ask for concrete examples.
7. What, if anything, would you return for?

Clarify consequential misconceptions only after recording them. No financial advice in the debrief.

## Iteration thresholds

- Any belief that Steady selected a personal fund, set an investment budget, certified readiness, guaranteed recovery or showed actual financial progress: flag immediately, locate the trigger, correct and retest.
- 3/5 struggling with the same common issue: prioritize a small fix.
- 2/5: assess severity and observed behavior before deciding.
- For a path-specific issue, report the actual denominator: 2/2 or 1/1. Do not dilute it into 2/5.
- Fewer than 4/5 completing onboarding independently under 180 seconds, or a common task successfully: investigate the cause rather than hiding slower successes.
- Increased confidence with incorrect explanations, or pressure at 4–5/5: treat as a trust concern.
- Isolated aesthetic preferences: document without expanding scope.

These are qualitative triage rules, not population estimates. Where feasible retest changed steps with two new people. Otherwise label changes “implemented, not user-validated.”

## Reporting and limits

After real sessions, report per-task independent/assisted/unsuccessful counts, individual onboarding times, branch denominators, confidence alongside comprehension, and three key observed issues with evidence, smallest fix and retest status. Do not invent results.

This study cannot establish affordability, suitability, real scheme terms, financial returns, readiness, durable learning, habit formation or population-wide Gen Z preferences. Completion records an interaction, not mastery. Supplied profiles, English-only content and moderator prompting limit conclusions. Persistence is local to one browser, not cross-device.

No user quote, completion rate or confidence improvement belongs in the submission until actually observed.
